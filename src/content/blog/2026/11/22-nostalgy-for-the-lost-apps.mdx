I am still tired, so this will be a short-ish post, stating that yes, I have a certain kind of nostalgia for the old apps, which I now consider to be lost, kinda.

![So artistic!](pixelfed:1002737110315023)

Above, is my first Instagram photo (selfie!) that I posted there as a test on March 15 2011. It is imported from [my Pixelfed](https://pixey.org/kizu), where I imported all my photo that I exported from Instagram a long time ago.

I no longer use Instagram. I no longer use Twitter. Even longer — Foursquare (that was an app!). LiveJournal — not anymore (ok, that is not an app… But — anyone remembers Semagic? lol.)

There was a moment in time, when those apps _felt good_. Like something new. Web 2.0, or just social web even before that.

_Everyone_ was there, at least somewhere. By “everyone”, I, of course, mean those nerds who were into this thing called Internet. And a few non-nerds, usually those who some other nerds were negging to join The Web.

Most of those networks were pseudonymous, not tied to real life unless you wanted it to, and not yet enshitified. Notably, I do not mention Facebook in the list above — screw it, I never liked it and only tried using it because some people who were _not_ on other apps went there. Not that it ended in any good way.

Maybe the model behind those apps was not sustainable, and we all were trapped by it, with the enshitification inevitable, exponential exposion of ads and promos, and people starting to see their accounts as a proxy for their social influence in some way.

I know people who still use all of these apps. Convenience, habits, not wanting to lose contact with others who are trapped in the same gooey substance, in the acid that sips inside and leaves husks of the most surface-level interactions.

Staying on these apps is an act of _conservatism_ in a way, maintaining _tradition_, keeping the old ways. No matter that the owners pour sevages on their heads, they will tolerate this, in the same way people tolerate authoritarian regimes. Yeah, there are some other parallels I could do here, but I should probably stop.

Anywho! I am happy that _now_ I am in networks that seem to be built with the ideals of resilience and resistance to those skewed ideas.

But that does not remove the past, the bright interactions that were happening, and all the good that could’ve been found there.

I think we need not to forget that feeling, and when building something new, not throwing away parts of the past that _were_ good, even though, inarguably, other parts, even then, were _not_.

Rambly ramble, my teacup is empty, time to wrap it up. That was the wrapping up, just in case.
